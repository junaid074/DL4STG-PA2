\section{Question 1 --- River Forecasting Proposal}

\subsection{1. Architecture diagram}

I sketched the pipeline as a line along the river, with side inputs joining where they physically belong:

\begin{verbatim}
  rain R1,R2 (history) ----\
  tributary T1 (history) ---+-- align to 10-min grid --> exogenous encoder
  reservoir releases -------/     (past + announced future)
                                         |
  levels S1..S4 (history) ---- per-station temporal encode
                                         |
              S1 --> S2 --> S3 --> S4   (message passing along the river)
                    ^ T1 joins before S2
                                         |
                         decode --> 6 steps ahead at S1..S4
\end{verbatim}

Rough tensor sizes (batch left out): levels $5\times L$ (S1--S4 plus T1), rain $2\times L$, releases $L+H$, station latents $4\times L\times d$, forecast $4\times H$. I use $L=36$ (6\,h), $H=6$ (1\,h), $d$ around 64. Rain/releases get projected and added at the stations they affect; releases hit S1 first.

\subsection{2. Assumptions}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item \textbf{A1.} After a bit of interpolation, everything sits on a shared 10-minute clock. That is normal for this kind of monitoring setup; it only affects alignment.
\item \textbf{A2.} The next hour of announced releases is known when we issue (the brief says so).
\item \textbf{A3.} Travel time is not a fixed constant --- it drifts with how high the river is. I keep a simple prior $\hat\tau_{ij}\propto\mathrm{dist}_{ij}$ and let the network correct it.
\item \textbf{A4.} The unmonitored tributary between S2 and S3 is just a residual inflow. I am not inventing a fake gauge for it.
\item \textbf{A5.} Six hours of history is enough for recent waves and release effects.
\item \textbf{A6.} Noise looks roughly additive per station, so Huber loss is fine. A full probabilistic flood model would be nice later, not required for this pitch.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{3. Forecasting task and data specification}

Every 10 minutes at issuance time $t_0$ I look at the last $L$ steps and predict $H=6$ future levels at S1--S4 only (one hour ahead).

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llp{5.4cm}}
\toprule
Name & Shape & What / when \\
\midrule
levels S1--S4, T1 & $B\times 5\times L$ & past, up to $t_0$ \\
rain R1, R2 & $B\times 2\times L$ & past only \\
releases & $B\times (L+H)$ & past + announced future \\
graph & edges + distances & fixed \\
output & $B\times 4\times H$ & future S1--S4 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

At issuance I do \emph{not} get future rain or future levels. I do get the announced releases. If a rain gauge is late I reuse the last reading and flip a missingness bit.

\subsection{4. Model computations and design justification}

Each station gets a short temporal encoder $z_i=\mathrm{Enc}_T(Y_i,e_i)$. Then a few hops along the river:
\[
\tilde z_i = z_i + \sum_{j\to i}
\alpha_{ji}\,\psi(z_j,z_i,\mathrm{dist}_{ji},\hat\tau_{ji}).
\]
With degree at most two this is cheap. I want $K\ge 3$ so S1 can actually reach S4. The head turns $(\tilde z_i, U_{\mathrm{future}})$ into six steps. Train with mean Huber over S1--S4 and the horizon.

Who affects whom: releases and upstream rain show up at S1, then hop down. T1 feeds the edge into S2. S3 never sees the missing tributary directly --- only whatever S2 carries plus a residual the net can learn (A4). The travel-time prior is a hint, not a hard delay line.

\subsection{5. Operation and exceptional conditions}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item \textbf{Missing readings.} Keep a mask, fill with the last value. If a station has been dark for a few steps, stop summing it into neighbours but still try to decode it from them.
\item \textbf{Delayed readings.} Do not wait. Issue on whatever is complete; late packets land in the next cycle.
\item \textbf{Announced future releases.} Pass them straight into the decoder as known inputs.
\item \textbf{Schedule vs actual.} When the real release differs, S1 notices on the next step and the error walks downstream. A tiny residual corrector on S1 is optional.
\end{itemize}
Four stations and $L=36$ is a small model --- easy to run every ten minutes on a normal CPU.

\subsection{6. Uncertainty, limitations, and additional information}

The unmonitored tributary can still surprise S3/S4 after a local storm; a cheap stage gauge there would help. Travel times change in flood, so the prior goes stale --- occasional discharge checks would let me retune $\hat\tau$. Rain away from R1/R2 is invisible; radar QPE over the basin would help. Without those extras the system still runs. It just leans harder on recent downstream levels and will be weaker on rare tributary-driven events.

\subsection*{Final model (symmetrised)}

For any trained $f$,
\[
\bar f(X)=\frac18\sum_{g\in G} g^{-1}\big(f(g(X))\big).
\]
Because $G$ is a finite group, reindexing the sum shows $\bar f(M(X))=M(\bar f(X))$ and the same for $R$, so $C_M=C_R=0$ exactly (up to float noise). I wrap \textbf{model D} (handout default). Table 5 already gave D a small 3$\times$ edge over C, and I want the exact symmetry.
